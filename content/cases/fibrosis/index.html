Case 2 · Cross-organ fibrosis

One question, two prompts: where the answers agreed, and where they did not

We asked OmiCairn the same fibrosis question twice: once as a single sentence, once as a detailed expert prompt. Both runs found conserved fibroblast matrix programs. They disagreed on the macrophage program and on the three-cell niche. The stricter, expert-specified criteria overturned the looser claim.

1 Input

The two prompts

The research direction was the same. What changed was how much the user specified up front.

Comprehensive prompt

Comprehensive prompt

“Research goal: Identify conserved cellular programs and recurrent multicellular fibrotic niches underlying fibrosis across human organs.”

  • Competing hypotheses required (the run tested five, H1–H5)
  • Cross-organ design: lung, liver, kidney, heart and skin when available
  • Spatial validation of niches wherever spatial data exist
  • Nine required tasks, a list of expected outputs, and explicit constraints

The kind of prompt a computational expert might write.

Read the full comprehensive prompt

Research goal: Identify conserved cellular programs and recurrent multicellular fibrotic niches underlying fibrosis across human organs.

You are asked to act as an omics co-scientist. Starting from this research goal, generate a hypothesis-driven discovery workflow to determine whether human fibrotic diseases across different organs share conserved cellular states, signaling circuits, and niche architectures.

Datasets and scope: Analyze available human single-cell or single-nucleus RNA-seq datasets from fibrotic and matched non-fibrotic tissues across multiple organs, prioritizing lung, liver, kidney, heart, and skin when available. If spatial transcriptomics data are available for any organ, use them to validate whether the inferred multicellular programs form spatially organized fibrotic niches.

Required tasks:

  1. Generate a set of competing biological hypotheses about conserved and organ-specific fibrotic programs.
  2. Harmonize cell-type annotations across organs and define comparable major cell classes, including fibroblasts, macrophages, endothelial cells, epithelial or parenchymal cells, T cells, and other immune or stromal populations.
  3. Identify disease-enriched fibroblast states and determine whether activated matrix-producing fibroblast programs are conserved across organs.
  4. Identify immune and vascular programs that recurrently co-occur with fibrotic fibroblast states, with particular attention to macrophage, endothelial, hypoxia, inflammatory, and extracellular-matrix remodeling programs.
  5. Distinguish conserved fibrotic core programs from organ-specific injury programs.
  6. Perform pathway/module scoring, differential abundance or differential expression analysis, ligand–receptor analysis, and cross-organ conservation analysis.
  7. Where spatial data are available, test whether conserved cell states co-localize into recurrent fibrotic niches.
  8. Prioritize candidate pan-fibrotic and organ-specific targets based on cross-organ conservation, cell-type specificity, ligand–receptor evidence, druggability, and translational relevance.
  9. Generate a final evidence-linked report with figures, ranked hypotheses, supporting and contradictory evidence, limitations, and proposed experimental validation strategies.

Expected outputs: a dependency-aware analysis tree showing each analytical step; a ranked list of conserved fibrotic hypotheses; a conserved versus organ-specific program matrix; cell-type and pathway-level figures; ligand–receptor or niche interaction networks; a candidate target prioritization table; a final discovery report written for biomedical researchers; all code, parameters, intermediate files, execution logs, and review results for reproducibility.

Important constraints: Do not assume that a program is universal unless it is supported across multiple organs and datasets. Clearly separate conserved, recurrent, organ-specific, and weakly supported findings. Report uncertainty and recommend follow-up validation experiments.

Why they disagreed

  • Organ coverage. Only the comprehensive run had heart and skin data. In heart, SPP1 went down, not up.
  • What was measured. The fuzzy run’s coupling metric shows that two cell fractions rise together across donors. That is real, but it does not show that a program is conserved, or that the cells sit next to each other.
  • What “conserved” meant. The expert prompt told OmiCairn not to call a program universal unless multiple organs and datasets supported it. The comprehensive run tested that with meta-analysis and spatial data. The fuzzy run compared organs descriptively.

How we word this

We do not say the two prompts “converged on” the macrophage program. They agreed that the state exists in several organs. They disagreed on whether it is a conserved program, and the stricter analysis did not support it.

4 Overlap

Only a quarter of the findings were shared

The fuzzy run reported 17 findings and the comprehensive run 23. Of 32 distinct findings, 8 were shared (25%), 9 came only from the fuzzy prompt (28%) and 15 only from the comprehensive prompt (47%).

5 The lesson

Stricter, expert-specified criteria overturned a looser exploratory claim

OmiCairn did not need a full protocol to start. But a confident headline from an exploratory prompt is a hypothesis, not a result.

  1. Exploratory prompts are useful for breadth

    From one sentence, OmiCairn chose datasets, built signatures and corrected itself across three kidney datasets. It recovered the main matrix signal and contributed 9 findings the expert prompt did not.

  2. Their headline claims need verification

    The fuzzy run’s “conserved niche” rested on three organs and no spatial data. With heart, skin and spatial tests added, the macrophage and niche claims were not supported.

  3. Experts change the answer, not only the depth

    The expert prompt defined what “conserved” must mean before any analysis ran. That definition is what turned an implied niche into a tested, and failed, hypothesis.

6 Limits

What this case does not show

  • One pair of runs. This is one question asked two ways, not a benchmark of prompting styles.
  • Counting findings is a judgment. The 17, 23 and 8 depend on how findings were itemized and matched (same cell type, program and direction).
  • Spatial evidence is thin. The niche test rests on lung and skin, which point in opposite directions. A kidney spatial dataset had no usable coordinates.
  • Lung dominates. Lung is the best-powered organ and drives much of the cross-organ signal in both runs. Effect sizes for many genes are highly heterogeneous across organs.
  • The fuzzy run’s coupling signal may still matter. Fibroblast and macrophage fractions do rise together across donors in lung and kidney. That may reflect a shared injury response; it is not evidence of a conserved, co-localized niche.
  • Targets are unverified. Druggability in the comprehensive run’s target ranking was curated and flagged for verification.
  • No experiments. Everything here is computational and hypothesis-generating. It has not been validated in the lab.
