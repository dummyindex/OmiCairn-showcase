Case 1 · Clear cell renal cell carcinoma

Nominating spatial resistance niches in ccRCC from one open-ended question

The user asked a broad question about treatment resistance and named no mechanism. OmiCairn planned and ran a multi-step analysis across single-cell, spatial and bulk clinical data, and nominated a set of candidate resistance niches. A separate laboratory check on patient biopsies supported the plausibility of the proposed tissue architecture. It did not show cause or treatment response.

1 The prompt

What the user asked

An open-ended natural-language request. It named a disease and the kinds of evidence to combine, but no predefined mechanism, gene or pathway.

2 What OmiCairn did

A dependency-aware workflow, built from the prompt

OmiCairn turned the request into an analysis tree: each step's outputs feed the next, and every step keeps its code, parameters and logs. Supporting agents retrieved literature and domain knowledge, reused earlier code, created new functions, fixed failing steps and reviewed jobs.

  1. Gather literature and data

    Retrieved a literature corpus and collected the relevant datasets: single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, TCGA-KIRC clinical data and therapy-response data.

  2. Annotate cell states

    Defined tumour, myeloid and T-cell states from single-cell data (n = 19), including SPP1+ tumour-associated macrophages and exhausted CD8 T cells.

  3. Map spatial niches

    Scored gene programmes on spatial transcriptomics sections (n = 24) and tested how they co-localize: HIF targets, macrophage, angiogenesis, collagen barrier and CD8 T-cell programmes.

    Scores from scanpy.tl.score_genes (Figure 5B)

  4. Infer ligand–receptor signalling

    Compared ligand–receptor interaction strength across four niche types: vascular remodelling, immunosuppressive border, metabolic barrier and immune-active.

  5. Test clinical association

    Compared survival in TCGA-KIRC between patients above and below the median expression of nine niche marker genes (Kaplan–Meier).

  6. Nominate follow-up strategies

    Ranked candidate resistance niches and proposed drug combinations that target them, as hypotheses for later testing (Supplementary Figure 5).

  7. Protein-level check on patient tissue

    Researchers stained ccRCC biopsies from treatment-sensitive and treatment-resistant tumours for CD44, PAX8, CD8, SPP1 and CD68, and quantified marker-positive cell fractions.

    Multiplex immunofluorescence; image analysis in Fiji; Wilcoxon test with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment

What it nominated for testing

These are untested hypotheses, not treatment recommendations

OmiCairn proposed drug combinations that would target the nominated niches. None was tested in this case: no drug was given and no response was measured. The manuscript states that the resistance model and the combinations are priorities for downstream validation, "not ... established mechanisms or clinical recommendations". This is not medical advice.

4 How it was checked

Two independent checks: patient survival and patient tissue

The spatial findings came from transcriptomic data. Two checks looked at the same niche from other angles: survival in a large bulk cohort, and protein staining in tissue from treated patients.

Survival association in TCGA-KIRC

For each of nine niche marker genes, patients were split at the median expression and survival was compared between the two groups. We list all nine, including those with no association.

Multiplex immunofluorescence on patient biopsies

Biopsy sections from ccRCC tumours that were sensitive (n = 17) or resistant (n = 13) to treatment were stained for CD44, PAX8 (tumour epithelium), CD8, SPP1 and CD68 (macrophages), with DAPI for nuclei. This was laboratory work done by researchers, outside the automated workflow.

In resistant tissue, CD44, SPP1 and CD68 signals were stronger. CD8+ cells were abundant but sat mainly in PAX8-low regions instead of inside PAX8+ tumour nests, consistent with an immune-excluded architecture.

5 Limits

Limits and what this case does not claim

The case shows that OmiCairn can turn an open question into a traceable set of testable hypotheses. It does not establish a mechanism.

  • Association, not cause.The spatial correlations, ligand–receptor inferences and survival splits are associations. The protein-level assessment "did not establish causality or therapeutic response" (manuscript).
  • Weak correlations.All three spatial correlations are small in size (rho between −0.09 and +0.21). Very small P values here come from many measurements.
  • No treatment was tested.The five combinations are nominated hypotheses. They are not clinical recommendations and must not be read as advice.
  • Simple survival analysis.Median splits, one gene at a time, with no reported adjustment for stage, grade or multiple testing. SPP1, the centre of the proposed niche, showed no survival difference.
  • Small tissue cohort.30 biopsies in two groups. The case description does not state which treatment or which response criteria defined the sensitive and resistant groups. The images in Figure 5E are selected representative examples.
  • Not a new discovery.SPP1+ macrophages co-localizing with CD44+ programmes have been reported before in several tumours. Recovering a known axis from an open prompt is a plausibility check of the workflow.
  • Not yet published.This case comes from the OmiCairn manuscript, which is in preparation. Treat it as preliminary.
  • Reported counts differ.The TCGA-KIRC cohort is n = 613 in the main text and n = 950 in the supplementary figure legend. We show both rather than choose one.
