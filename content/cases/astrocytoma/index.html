Case 3 · IDH-mutant astrocytoma · Expert in the graph

Three rounds of expert feedback raised concordance with a reference study from 6/17 to 9/17

OmiCairn analysed the publicly available CGGA subset (DataSet ID CCell_4083) of the IDH-mutant astrocytoma cohort reported by Tang et al. Each round was compared with 17 findings of the original study, with evaluators from the reference study team. The OmiCairn system stayed the same; only the human input grew.

What this gain measures

Expert input accumulated across rounds, so the rise from 6/17 to 9/17 measures how well OmiCairn turned feedback into targeted re-analysis. It is not an increase in autonomous capability. The Round 3 gains came mainly from analyses that followed an expert hint and used the published subtype labels: OmiCairn executed expert-directed tests correctly; it did not rediscover the immune-hot subtype on its own.

1 The data

35 tumours, five omics layers, uneven coverage

The dataset is the publicly available CGGA subset (DataSet ID CCell_4083) of the cohort reported by Tang et al.: 35 IDH-mutant astrocytoma samples with EPIC DNA methylation, bulk RNA-seq, single-cell RNA-seq, phosphoproteomics and proteomics, plus clinical annotations. Not every sample has every layer. Single-cell RNA-seq covers 18 of the 35.

No subtype structure or biomarker model was imposed before the analysis.

Reference study

2 The prompt

An open-ended question, with rules against assuming the answer

OmiCairn was asked to act as both an executor and a critical reviewer, and to explore the molecular and cellular mechanisms behind tumour heterogeneity, progression, recurrence and treatment resistance. The task covered subtype discovery, the tumour microenvironment, clinical associations and biomarker prioritization. It did not name the reference study’s subtypes or biomarkers.

The prompt also asked for spatial and longitudinal analyses where such data existed. The public subset has neither, which matters for what could and could not be recovered.

From the task prompt

“This project is data-driven. Do not assume any subtype, pathway, biomarker, or mechanism before analysis.”

“Treat associations as hypothesis-generating unless independently validated.”

“Report alternative explanations and negative findings.”

How concordance was counted

Concordance is the share of a fixed set of 17 reference findings that OmiCairn recovered with matching direction, modality support and biological interpretation. Partial and trend-level recoveries count. A strict count of full recoveries alone would be lower in every round (3, 3 and 7 in the table below).

What OmiCairn could not recover, and why

According to the manuscript, most of the eight unrecovered findings depended on data that were unavailable or could not be retrieved, or on different methodological choices. The per-finding reasons below come from the case’s supplementary comparison.

6 depended on data

  • Single-cell cell states (AC / OPC / MES / NPC). Needs the single-cell data. Single-cell RNA-seq exists for 18 of the 35 samples, but the download failed in every round (incomplete or corrupted file).
  • CytoTRACE differentiation trajectory. Same single-cell retrieval failure.
  • Gemistocyte histology. Needs whole-slide images and spatial data, which were not used. A bulk marker score was computed instead (see below).
  • Visium HD spatial organisation. Spatial transcriptomics is not part of the public CCell_4083 subset.
  • Longitudinal evolution across matched primary and recurrent tumours. The public subset has one time point per patient.
  • Protein-level validation in an independent proteomics cohort. No independent proteomics cohort was publicly available; OmiCairn validated at the RNA level in CGGA_693 instead.

2 reflect a different method

  • MOFA latent factors. OmiCairn integrated the omics layers with a similarity-network approach instead.
  • The reference study’s tree-based multi-modal classifier. OmiCairn built a signature-based classifier instead.

A different method is a choice, not a missing data type. Whether the reference study’s methods would give the same answer on this subset was not tested.

Single-cell data existed but were not analysed

The single-cell layer is part of the dataset (18 of 35 samples). It was missing from the analysis because the download failed, not because the data do not exist. Three unrecovered findings depend fully or partly on it. Whether they would be recovered with the data in hand is untested.

Where OmiCairn and the reference disagreed

In Rounds 1 and 2 the plasma-cell signal appeared highest in the AFM subtype rather than IME; the supplementary comparison attributes this to IME not yet being separated from AFM. With the published labels in Round 3 it moved to IME as a non-significant trend. Round 3 also produced a bulk gemistocytic marker score that was highest in PPR rather than IME. The reference study linked gemistocytes to IME using histology and single-cell data, which OmiCairn could not use, so the two observations rest on different evidence.

7 External cohort

Subtype signatures tested in CGGA_693

In Round 3, OmiCairn applied a four-subtype expression signature to the independent CGGA_693 cohort. Tumours assigned to PPR and to IME both had worse survival there.

This validation is at the RNA level. The reference study validated its protein-based classification in an independent proteomics cohort, which was not publicly available, so the two validations are not equivalent.
