About this site

This site presents what OmiCairn produced: a 57-run neurodegeneration research campaign and the three case studies of the OmiCairn manuscript. This page lists where the code and data live, how to cite them, the licenses, and what the results can and cannot be used for.

Data on this site

The campaign pages are built from the campaign release. You can download the same data in machine-readable form.

Hypotheses
All 179 hypotheses with verdict, evidence quote and adjudication note: hypotheses.json or the sanitized hypotheses.csv. Browse them in the hypothesis explorer.
Runs
All 57 runs with their status, hypothesis counts and research question: runs.json, or the sanitized runs.csv.
Campaign summary
The headline numbers and how each was computed: campaign.json.
Reports
The final report of each of the 23 runs that produced one, linked from each run’s page under Campaign: 18 as PDF and 5 rendered from Markdown.
Case studies
Text and figures from the OmiCairn manuscript (in preparation). Figures are marked as such.

What was changed for this site

Run identifiers were renumbered (<topic>-rN) and run dates and times were removed. In hypothesis text, names of run-internal files and stage folders were replaced with plain descriptions, and three report bodies say “this report” for the system’s own output. No numbers, statistics, verdicts or hypotheses were changed. The report PDFs are published exactly as released. Some of them call themselves a “paper”, call the system’s internal multi-agent review “peer review”, or cite internal stage folders by number; those folders are not shipped.

This site makes no requests to other servers, sets no cookies and has no analytics. The only thing it stores is your color theme, in your own browser.

About the outputs

How to read the outputs

  • Generated by an autonomous system. The campaign reports and verdicts were produced end to end by OmiCairn, without manual edits to their scientific content.
  • Internal review. The review inside each run is a multi-agent review by reviewer agents and a claim-review agent, which are part of the system. Passing it shows that a run went through the pipeline, not that its conclusions are valid.
  • Hypotheses, not recommendations. Treatment combinations named in the case studies are hypotheses nominated for testing.
  • Known imperfections are left in place. Many reports contain unresolved inconsistencies, such as titles stronger than the results or counts that differ between sections. They were recorded but not corrected, because correcting them would mean rewriting the system’s output. Read each report next to its verdicts and adjudication notes.
  • Third-party data keep their own terms. Datasets used by the runs remain under the licenses and terms of their providers. No patient-level information is shown on this site.
